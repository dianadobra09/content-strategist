# Threads: what works

Studied 2026-10-11 to adapt LinkedIn posts for Threads. Everything here describes other accounts or Meta's guidance. Nothing is measured on the author's account, so treat it as starting defaults to test.

## Summary

- Posts are short: about 30 words is typical. Length alone didn't decide which posts did best.
- Text alone is the most common format, but images are common too. In this niche they didn't beat text in total engagement; Buffer's larger study found higher engagement rates for images and video.
- What did best for each creator: a personal moment with a specific detail, a quick reaction to tech news, a plain opinion, one concrete observation from work, a short demo.
- What did worst: a link in the post itself, quote posts, generic tips and lists, generic questions.
- Meta says replies bring almost half of all views, and its ranking predicts replies and uses the replies under replies. Answering replies is part of how a post spreads.
- Meta recommends writing for Threads rather than cross-posting.

## Evidence and limits

**Top search results.** 25 searches on tech, AI and startup terms (AI tools, Claude, Claude Code, LLM, AI agents, vibe coding, software engineer, coding, startup, founder, building in public, SaaS, data privacy, AI writing and others), read logged out on threads.com on 2026-10-11. 437 unique original posts; 147 were in English and at most 180 days old, from 141 authors. The Top tab's ranking isn't public and mixes relevance, engagement and age.

**Creator profiles.** The latest 15 threads from 28 English accounts posting about tech, AI or startups (49 to 330K followers, median about 14K): 385 original posts, 2–365 days old, median age 24 days. Each post is compared with its own author's median engagement, so small and large accounts count equally. Engagement is likes, replies (minus the author's own first reply), reposts and quotes.

Counts are snapshots at capture. There are no impressions, follower gains or clicks. 15 posts per creator is small, post ages differ, and the creators were picked from search results and known names, not at random. These are patterns, not causes. The per-post data isn't included in this repo.

## What the posts look like

| | Top search, English (147) | Creators (385) |
|---|---|---|
| Median length | 175 characters, 31 words | 185 characters, 31 words |
| Up to 200 characters | 59% | 53% |
| Over 300 characters | 21% | 31% |
| Text only | 61% | 34% |
| Image or carousel | 29% | 39% |
| Video | 7% | 18% |
| Link in the post | 7% | 13% |
| Ends with a question | 7% | 8% |
| Topic tag | 26% | 7% |

A quarter of creator posts (26%) had the author's own reply attached, and 13% a chain of two or more.

More than half (54%) of the recent top results for these English search terms weren't in English: a quarter were Malay or Indonesian, the rest Portuguese, Russian, Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese and others. The English tech conversation is a smaller part of Threads than the search terms suggest.

A fifth (21%) of the English top results were networking ("let's connect"), hiring or "Dear algo" posts. They collect replies (median 1.2 replies per like, against 0.25 for other posts) and carry little else.

## What did better within each creator's posts

- **Length didn't decide it.** Each creator's top three posts had a median of 174 characters; the rest had 187. Short posts (up to 200 characters) did better than long ones (over 300) for 5 creators and worse for 7.
- **Media didn't decide it.** Text did better than images for 8 creators and worse for 8. Video was mixed.
- **A link in the post did worse.** 39% of posts with a link beat their author's median, against 48% without; only 5 of 16 creators' link posts averaged above their median. Links in the author's own reply didn't show this, but only 3 creators did both.
- **Quote posts did worse.** Only 1 of 10 creators' quote posts averaged above their median.
- **Lists did worse** (3 of 9 creators), and generic advice ("become reliable", "6 tiny UX improvements") was among each creator's lowest posts.
- **Questions brought replies, not more engagement.** Posts ending with a question had more replies per like (0.28 against 0.08) and no more total engagement (4 creators better, 3 worse).
- **Topic tags looked helpful**, with 5 of 7 creators' tagged posts above their median. Few creators used them.

Best posts relative to their author's median: a career plan that changed over ten years; after 36 years in the US, worse at both English and French; a one-line reaction to a $500 ChatGPT plan; "the cheapest form of market research: search '[your industry] spreadsheet template'"; a short video of a photobooth built from emoji. Lowest: bare link shares, motivational lines, tips lists, generic questions ("What are the best businesses to be in a post-AGI world?") and self-promotion.

## What Meta says

- **Limits.** 500 characters per post, emojis included; up to 20 photos and videos; videos up to 5 minutes; one topic tag per post. The feed shows the whole post, with no "more" cut (checked on the web feed). A text attachment holds up to 10,000 characters but shows as a gray box readers tap to open. Text can be edited for 15 minutes after posting.
- **Ranking.** The Threads feed card (Transparency Center, updated 7 March 2025) predicts likes, replies, clicks on the post or profile, time spent, and scrolling past. It uses engagement on replies to replies in the first hours. Reposts, quotes and follows aren't on the list.
- **Replies.** "Replies account for almost half of the views on Threads", and "replying to anyone is just as important as posting your own original content" (creator page, 2024 data).
- **What limits reach.** Content that isn't recommendable (clickbait, engagement bait, contests) and unoriginal content.
- **Writing for Threads.** Cross-posting is "fine if you are just getting started, but creating text-based content specifically for Threads is a better strategy." In October 2025, Connor Hayes, head of Threads, said ranking would favor timely events and people who drive conversations, and that posting directly to Threads should get more reach. No Meta statement says Threads detects or demotes text copied from LinkedIn or X; Instagram's originality rules apply to Instagram only.
- **Links.** Adam Mosseri, November 2024: "We don't downrank links, but we don't place much value on p(click) and people don't like and comment on links much." June 2025: links "have been working much better for more than a month now." So a link isn't penalized as such; link posts get fewer likes and replies, which matches the creator data.
- **Frequency.** Meta recommends at least two to five posts a week (2024 data). The author's cadence is their decision (`strategy/positioning.md`).
- **Viewer controls.** "Dear Algo" posts (February 2026) and Your Algo (June 2026) let viewers ask for more or less of a topic. They change the viewer's own feed; they aren't a way to reach readers.
- **Audience.** 500 million monthly users (June 2026) and 150 million daily (October 2025). In the US, 8% of adults use Threads against 21% for X (Pew, 2025), and Stack Overflow's 2025 developer survey didn't list it. Expect a smaller tech audience than on LinkedIn or X.

## What larger studies say

- **Formats.** Buffer, 45M+ posts across platforms (March 2026): median engagement rates on Threads were 5.55% for video, 4.55% for images, 2.79% for text and 2.34% for links. That is engagement per view across Buffer's users, not total engagement in this niche, which is why it can differ from the creator comparison.
- **Replies.** Buffer, 128K+ Threads posts compared within the same accounts (November 2025): posts where the author replied to comments had 42% more engagement, the largest gain of six platforms. A correlation.
- **Timing.** Buffer, 2.5M posts (February 2026): weekday mornings, 6 to 11 am local time, did best; evenings after 6 pm did worst; Saturday was the weakest day.
- **Length.** No published study of post length on Threads was found; the creator comparison above is the only evidence here.

## What this means for Threads versions

Defaults to test, not rules.

- Write the Threads version for Threads: one point per post, in the author's words, usually their moment or their opinion with the one detail that makes it theirs. The 500-character limit is a ceiling.
- Text alone by default. Use a real visual when it is the point: their photo, a screenshot, a meme they picked, a factual diagram.
- No first reply with source links, and no link in the post unless the author asks.
- Keep the author's own question when they have one. Don't add one for replies.
- One topic tag when an existing tag fits.
- Skip reply bait ("let's connect", "Dear algo", "drop your link", "what are you building?"), quote posts as the main version, Unicode bold and listicles.
- The author's replies to replies matter more on Threads than on the other platforms.

After ten published Threads posts, compare them in `outputs/log.md` and update this file. Threads Insights split reach between followers and non-followers (since September 2026); log both.
