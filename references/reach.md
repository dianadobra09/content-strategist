# Reach hypotheses and angle tags

These tags describe an angle. They do not rank its value or predict engagement.
Select for the reader, the author's contribution and evidence first (`generate-content`). Use `reach: none` when no tag helps.

## Evidence status

Keep four things distinct: the author's editorial preferences, creator-reported results, observed sample patterns and results measured on the author's account.
A clear opening and link-free LinkedIn comments are editorial choices. They are not established algorithm mechanisms.
Creator studies suggest tests; they do not prove that a tactic caused reach. Related creators are not independent confirmations.

## Tags

- **new idea:** a subject the author has not covered. Check previous posts, but do not penalize a useful follow-up with new evidence.
- **step-by-step / cheat sheet:** the reader can use real steps or rules. Choose the visual through `strategy/visuals.md`.
- **hot take:** a belief the author actually holds, backed by their reasoning or experience. Keep necessary qualifications; do not force disagreement.
- **whole system:** a bounded workflow the author uses, including its inputs, steps, outputs and limits. It can use a factual diagram or real artifacts.
- **early shift:** a practice the author has tried while it is emerging. Verify its date and state what their test establishes.
- **newsjack:** a timely event with a consequence or use case from the author's work. Read the primary source. Later hands-on evidence can still be useful.
- **brandjack:** a known company's action examined through the author's experience. Use first-party facts; do not imply a relationship or product comparison they have not authorized.
- **namejack:** a person's work or statement that the author addresses. Quote accurately and link the source. Mention them only when relevant.
- **trend:** a current format adapted to a supported point the author wants to make. Skip it if it weakens their voice or requires an exaggerated claim.
- **giveaway:** a real, relevant resource the author uses and can share. Link directly; never require a comment to receive it. No quota or promised multiplier.

These options never override the voice rules, category purpose or `strategy/product.md`. Familiarity is useful when it helps the intended reader understand.

## Creator material the skills cite

Each is one account's self-report. Use it as a test, not a rule.

- **A creator's X and Threads playbook** (a PDF guide, 2026): credits opinions over teaching on X, questions for replies and statements for shares, volume, and one reply with a link under posts that took off. It is the source of the Group callout, Fear line and Pick a side formats, of "lead with a view on X", of "Threads readers are more skeptical of AI" and of "raw thoughts every two or three days". X's published ranking code does weight a predicted reply at 5.0 against 0.66 for a like (github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm, checked 2026-10-17).
- **A LinkedIn creator's article on why posts spread** (October 2026): emotion, not information, makes a post spread. It shows no data. It is the source of `references/psychology.md`.
- **A pasted article on LinkedIn text posts** (October 2026): LinkedIn shows text posts bigger, a short one in full with no "...more" and a long one with about 7 lines before it. Like LinkedIn's other launches it is an A/B test, so not every account has it. It is the source of the text-only formats in `references/formats.md`.
