## Human and relatable

Options, not requirements for every post. Use only details present in the author's material.

**Show the moment**

- Start in a real moment: where, when, with whom.
- Use time markers and the small details only someone who was there would know: the hour, the place, how old you were.
- Use an exact number only when it is known and relevant. Keep honest approximations when that is what the source supports.

**Show the people**

- Name people, quote what they said (with their permission) and thank them by name.
- Let users, customers or teammates be the hero. The product comes late, if at all.
- Use the words users and colleagues actually said.

**Show yourself**

- Say what you think, in first person: "I keep thinking about..."
- Admit what you got wrong or used to believe.
- Be a little funny about yourself, the way you'd say it out loud.
- Give honest updates, even boring ones.
- Give away the exact thing: the setup, the template, the numbers.

## Rules

Starter rules. Replace or extend them with the author's own as `train-voice` learns them. Personal style choices go here too, for example "start every post with a lowercase letter" or "write in blocks with single line breaks".

- The body comes from the author's words. If they only gave a title or one line, build it from their lines in other ideas, or ask for 2-3 raw sentences first. Posts composed from scratch read as AI.
- When the author wrote the text, keep it. Fix typos and grammar, cut repetition, and keep their hook, asides and closing question. Don't restructure it, except to lay a LinkedIn post out as in `linkedin-post` step 4 with their words unchanged.
- Join thoughts with commas, "and", "so", "but". No short fragments for rhythm.
- Keep sentences short: split one that runs past about 15 words into two full sentences. Short still means a complete sentence, not a fragment.
- Keep the author's asides (TODO: list theirs, for example "I think", "personally", "I'll be honest").
- Use the plainest accurate word. Real technical terms are fine when they're the precise name (race condition, idempotent, context window). Fancy words as upgrades to ordinary words are not.
- Explain how or why something happens in plain words: one cause and effect per sentence or → line, no legal or technical wording, no "it" or "that" the reader has to trace back.
- No analogies, metaphors, mirrored lines or clever inversions for decoration.
- One register: a person talking to peers. Direct and warm. No hype, no editorializing.
- Fix spelling and grammar. Don't smooth the author's sentences into generic polished English.
- Read-aloud test: would the author say this sentence to a colleague? If it sounds like showing off, rewrite it.
- Research and statistics only when the author asks for them or the post is built on a finding. Otherwise their own situation is the example. When a number is used: one, rounded the way they'd say it, in their words, with no sample size in brackets.

## Staying recognizable over time

Subject choices, judgments and experiences matter as much as punctuation or layout.
Keep the author's natural uncertainty, interests, asides and recurring concerns. Do not add personality traits from a creator or a template.
A clear post needs no mandatory joke, confession, disagreement or personal scene.
Use raw notes, voice memos and eligible samples to learn. Publication metrics do not decide their voice.
A repeated edit suggests a preference in context; it does not automatically justify a universal ban.
