# Kill list

Never in a post. `voice-check` scans every draft against this list. Add to it whenever the author rejects something.

## Punctuation and formatting

- Em dashes (—) and en dashes (–). Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses.
- Bold or italic Unicode letters (𝗹𝗶𝗸𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀).
- Emoji as bullets or decoration (🚀 ✅ 👉 💡 🔥 👇).
- Perfect formatting: headed or numbered sections, tidy parallel bullets, bold. On X, one line per step is fine when it's a real sequence.
- Bullet lists for things that aren't lists.
- Hashtag blocks.
- Title Case headings inside posts.
- Exception for LinkedIn posts: the layout in `linkedin-post` step 4, with short paragraphs, blank lines between them, and a → list under a label line for a real list. Hashtags, "save this" or "send this" lines and 👇 stay out.
- Threads posts take the same short paragraphs with blank lines (`threads-post`).

## Words

leverage, utilize, delve, unlock, unleash, harness, elevate, empower, supercharge, revolutionize, transformative, game-changer, seamless, effortless, cutting-edge, next-level, next-generation, groundbreaking, innovative, landscape, realm, tapestry, journey (for work), navigate (for anything but maps), foster, crucial, pivotal, vital, paramount, testament, showcase, underscore, streamline, synergy, holistic, vibrant, profound, intricate, nuanced, comprehensive, meticulous, ever-evolving, fast-paced, superpower, secret sauce, north star, move the needle, deep dive, unpack, mind-blowing, insane, massive (for things that aren't big)

Swear words on LinkedIn, even when the author's notes have them: "screwed", not the stronger word.

Allowed when it's the precise technical term: "optimize a query", "a key in the map", "robust to bad input".

## Openers

- "In today's fast-paced world", "In the age of AI", "In a world where"
- "I'm thrilled / excited / humbled to share"
- "Let's talk about", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "Here's the truth", "Here's the deal:"
- "BREAKING:", "biggest ever", "(READ THAT AGAIN)"
- "Hot take:" as a label, and "Unpopular opinion:" on LinkedIn. On X and Threads, "unpopular opinion:" can open a belief the author holds that really splits people (a test). Hot takes are fine: the claim itself is the first line (`references/reach.md`).
- "Most people think X. They're wrong." The honest version is allowed: a specific belief the reader holds, then the author's evidence (the belief-change post in `references/psychology.md`).
- "Stop doing X."
- "Imagine..."
- "As a <job title>, ..."
- A question the post then answers ("Ever wondered why...?")
- "X is dead.", except in a trend post while that format is going around (`references/reach.md`).
- "I'll be the first to admit"
- "this is my first post here", "hi, I'm <name>": no intro posts, unless the author wants one (for example on launch day).

## Constructions

- "Not X, it's Y" and its family: "It's not about X, it's about Y", "X isn't just A, it's B", "Less X, more Y"
- Lists of three for rhythm. Use the real number of items.
- Reveal lines: "The result?", "The kicker?", "The best part?", "Here's why:", "Here's what I learned:", "Spoiler:", "Plot twist:"
- Short fragments for rhythm: "It takes time." "Every message, every post."
- Mirrored lines and inversions: "a missed span is a leaked span", "only then does the request go out".
- Stock drama fragments: "Then it hit me." "Game over." "Mind. Blown." A real punchline is fine ("absolutely not.").
- Line breaks for fake suspense before the point.
- "-ing" tails that add fake depth: "..., highlighting the importance of X."
- Stacked hedges: "might potentially", "it could be argued".
- Calling the same thing by three different names.
- "From X to Y" when X and Y aren't a range.
- A last line that restates the post ("The lesson? Ship small.").
- Report-summary sentences: a statistic, a comparison ("compared with"), a trend clause ("that gap has halved since...") and the sample size in brackets, all in one sentence.
- Moral closers: "Remember:", "At the end of the day", "The bottom line", "Ultimately", "What works for me is...", "the other way around".

## Endings

- "Thoughts?", "Agree?", "What do you think? 👇", "Let me know in the comments"
- Generic "How are you using X?" questions. Ask something specific readers can answer about themselves.
- Either/or questions built for engagement ("do you X, or just Y?") and long, engineered questions. A closing question is short and plain, the way the author would ask a colleague. On X, an either/or question is fine when the author has their own answer and gives it in the replies (a test).
- "Follow me for more", "Repost if this helped ♻️", "Save this for later"
- "Comment 'X' and I'll send you..."
- A question nobody needs to answer. When the author ends with a real question, keep theirs.

## Tone

Academic, overly emotional, passive, silly, empty, jargon-heavy, trend-chasing, overly enthusiastic, aggressive, arrogant, feature-dumping. Anything that sounds like a generic AI company trying to impress people with technology.

A newsjack, brandjack, namejack or trend post isn't trend-chasing when it carries the author's own use case or view (`references/reach.md`).

## Worn-out topics

Reject generic versions of these topics. A concrete firsthand example, useful explanation or supported trade-off can qualify:

- AI will or won't replace engineers; "people using AI will replace you"
- Prompt tips and prompt lists
- "AI is just a tool"
- The future of work
- Vibe coding, good or bad, in general
- "I used AI to write this"
- Model A vs model B on launch day
- "Agents will change everything"; AGI timelines
- Slogans and explainers everyone has posted: "context engineering is the new prompt engineering", "MCP explained", "RAG is dead"
- Productivity hacks, "ship fast", "done is better than perfect"
- "Communication is key", "be authentic"
- Generic founder-journey lessons; "why you should build in public"
- Imposter syndrome, hustle or burnout takes without a specific story
- Reactions to someone else's launch that only restate it. A newsjack or brandjack adds a use case or consequence from the author's work (`references/reach.md`).

## Content

- Invented stories, numbers, users, quotes or results. Hard rule.
- Vague sources: "studies show", "experts say", "many people". Every external finding needs its primary source.
- Fake vulnerability and humblebrags.
- Advice with no example ("communicate clearly", "ship fast").
- Hype or doom about AI.
- Selling the product in the post: a hard sell, testimonial lines, a feature list or a call to action in the body. Naming it once, plainly, where it fits the post's problem is fine.
- Anything `strategy/product.md` lists under claims to avoid.

## Product copy

Product posts that read like an ad: testimonial lines ("since I use <product>, I no longer have to worry about..."), lists of supported apps ("in Slack, Gmail, Notion and more"), "See it in action below", and the question → product fixes it → call to action shape. Mention the product once, plainly, or let a real recording carry it.

Banned product phrases: "AI-powered", "world's first", plus TODO (from `strategy/product.md`).
