in 2024, Google paid $2.7B to get 2 people back. the company they left behind is now bankrupt with $57M in cash.

Character.AI is a very popular startup where you can build characters and chat with them. I would describe it as an AI Sims.

it was valued at over $1 billion at its peak, with about 20 million monthly users last year. I've never used it, but I am interested in what's going on.

for starters, their monetization isn't great. the main plan is a flat $9.99 a month, but the costs are usage based.

so the most active users are the most expensive to serve, which doesn't scale well.

in August it made $5.3 million, while compute alone cost about $7 million a month. The court filings blame the costs and the lawsuits.

but I think the real reason it collapsed is that it lost its 2 founders, Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas.

for context, Noam Shazeer co-wrote "Attention Is All You Need", the paper that introduced the transformer. almost all generative AI is built on it today: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude...

he and Daniel left Google in 2021, when it wouldn't release their chatbot, but in 2024, Google hired them both back with a $2.7 billion licensing deal.

perhaps Google wanted to buy the company, but couldn't for legal reasons?

what was left behind was a company that lost its vision of "personalized superintelligence", stopped building its own models and moved to open-weight ones.

so let's do the math: $2.7 billion vs a $20 million bid from Disney. The founders were worth about 135 times what's left of the startup.

less than a month before the bankruptcy, Disney hired its CEO as CTO, and most of its staff.

Google's deal was one of the first of many acquihires in the last couple of years. a big company licenses the tech and hires the founders, without buying the startup. Google did the same with Windsurf in 2025, for $2.4 billion.

the startups left behind often struggle to survive. since 2024, Character.AI has been through 2 acquihires and 3 CEOs.

for me, it shows how easy it is for a billion-dollar company to go broke, and just how important leadership is.
