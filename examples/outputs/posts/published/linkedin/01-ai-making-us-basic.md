using AI is making us basic.

i know there’s already a lot of chatter about this, but I think it’s worth drawing attention to the subject.

how it works:
→ an LLM is trained on next-token prediction. It reads some text and guesses the next word (a token, technically)
→ for every possible next word it calculates a probability, based on its training data. Then it usually picks one of the most likely ones
→ after that, it’s fine-tuned on human ratings, and people tend to rate the familiar answer higher
→ so the common answer gets even more weight, and the variety drops

researchers call this mode collapse.

a simple example: ask an AI to pick a random number between 1 and 10. People already lean towards 7, and these models learned from text people wrote. In a study last year, GPT-4o mini, Gemini and Phi-4 picked 7 about 8 times out of 10. That’s far more often than people do.

that doesn’t matter much for a random number, but it does for ideas. Most of us ask the same few models. In one study, readers rated stories written with ideas from GPT-4 as more creative. Those stories were also more similar to each other.

it’s also easy to stop questioning the answer. Microsoft and Carnegie Mellon surveyed knowledge workers about this. The more they trusted the AI, the less critical thinking they did. The ones who trusted their own skills questioned it more.

so what should we do?
→ in my case, when I want ideas from it, I ask for five answers with how likely each one is
→ that way it shows the less likely ones too
→ researchers at Stanford and Northeastern found this makes creative writing up to twice as varied

what other tips to avoid this do you have?
