@Kyle Poyar says the top 10% of AI power users drive more than 70% of token use. on a flat subscription, they pay the same as everyone else.

so subscription pricing may be deprecated for products that actively use LLMs.

we're debating pricing for ours right now. what it costs us depends on how much each customer uses it, so a subscription makes it hard to make projections.

GitHub moved Copilot to usage-based billing in June for a similar reason:
→ a quick chat question and a multi-hour coding session cost the user the same
→ GitHub said that model was "no longer sustainable"

so is usage-based pricing a better alternative? but doesn't it affect the customer experience, which is what I care about most?

Kyle also surveyed 230 software and AI companies this year:
→ the usage-based ones say their revenue is hard to forecast too
→ the most common choice is a subscription plus usage (37%, up from 25% a year earlier)
→ those companies are generally the happiest with their pricing
→ but they struggle to explain it to customers

if you've priced an AI product, what did you pick, and how did your customers react?
