from today, AI generated text in the EU has to be detectable as AI, and personally, I think it’s both good and bad news.

the rule now covers products already on the market too.

why I think it’s great:
→ while I don’t personally suffer from AI generated content, I use AI consistently to correct what I’ve already written
→ but I can definitely see how it’s terrible for writers and other creative professionals
→ it doesn’t seem fair that people with less skill can produce work 10x faster using AI, while genuine creative writing risks being left behind
→ if this leads to people losing gigs, income or creativity, I think it’s a real problem

why I’m frustrated with the new regulation 😅

I’ve been dealing with LLMs quite a bit lately, and I have to say, implementing the technology to support this is no walk in the park.

under the EU AI Act:
→ providers of generative AI systems now need to make generated content machine readable and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated
→ the rule started on 2 August 2026 for new AI systems
→ from 2 December 2026 it also covers the ones that were already on the market
→ AI that only does standard editing, like fixing grammar and spelling, is exempt

one way to make text detectable is to give users a way to test whether a piece of text was generated by your models, which could be as simple as a page with an input field that sends a request to your servers to check.

anyone else building with LLMs dealing with this?

Alt text 2: EU AI Act, Article 50: AI that generates text, images, audio or video has to mark its output as AI-generated, in a machine-readable way. From 2 August 2026 for AI systems put on the market from that day; from 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market before then. Exempt: AI that only does standard editing, like fixing grammar and spelling.
