## Human and relatable (top priority)

Options learned from two creators' posts, not requirements for every post. Use only details present in Diana's material.

**Show the moment**

- Start in a real moment: where, when, with whom.
- Use time markers and the small details only someone who was there would know: the hour, the place, how old you were.
- Use an exact number only when it is known and relevant. Keep honest approximations when that is what the source supports.

**Show the people**

- Name people, quote what they said (with their permission) and thank them by name.
- Let users, customers or teammates be the hero. The product comes late, if at all.
- Use the words users and colleagues actually said.

**Show yourself**

- Say what you think, in first person: "I keep thinking about..."
- Admit what you got wrong or used to believe.
- Be a little funny about yourself. One aside in brackets, the way you'd say it out loud.
- Give honest updates, even boring ones.
- Give away the exact thing: the setup, the template, the numbers.

## Rules

From Diana's feedback on the first drafts (2026-10-07): "I can still feel the AI", "start with a small letter", "don't format them so perfectly".

- Start every post with a lowercase letter.
- Don't format perfectly. Write in blocks with single line breaks and few blank lines. Uneven paragraphs are fine. No headers, no numbered sections, no bold, no tidy bullet lists unless Diana wrote them. LinkedIn posts use the layout of her ready posts instead: short paragraphs with blank lines between them, and → lists for real lists (`linkedin-post` step 4; her edits, 2026-10-11 to 2026-10-13). Threads posts use the same short paragraphs (`threads-post`; Diana, 2026-10-13).
- The body comes from Diana's words. If Diana only gave a title or one line, build it from Diana's lines in other ideas, or ask for 2-3 raw sentences first. Posts I compose from scratch read as AI (Diana, twice on 2026-10-07).
- When Diana wrote the text, keep it. Fix typos and grammar, cut repetition, and keep Diana's hook, asides and closing question. Don't restructure it, except to lay a LinkedIn post out as in `linkedin-post` step 4 with her words unchanged.
- Join thoughts the way Diana does, with commas, "and", "so", "but". No short fragments for rhythm.
- Keep sentences short: split one that runs past about 15 words into two full sentences (Diana, 2026-10-11, on two product posts: "make them in shorter sentences"). Short still means a complete sentence, not a fragment.
- Keep Diana's asides: "Really.", "I think", "personally", "I'll be honest", "in all honesty", "guess what:", "for sure", "etc."
- Use the plainest accurate word. Real technical terms are fine when they're the precise name (race condition, idempotent, context window). Fancy words as upgrades to ordinary words are not.
- Explain how or why something happens in plain words: one cause and effect per sentence or → line, no legal or technical wording, no "it" or "that" the reader has to trace back (Diana, 2026-10-13 on a comment: "I DON'T UNDERSTAND IIT"; 2026-10-14 on a post's why list: "make ... simpler to understand").
- No analogies, metaphors, mirrored lines or clever inversions for decoration.
- One register: a person talking to peers. Direct and warm. No hype, no editorializing.
- Fix spelling and grammar. Don't smooth Diana's sentences into generic polished English.
- Read-aloud test: would Diana say this sentence to a colleague in a code review? If it sounds like showing off, rewrite it.
- Research and statistics only when Diana asks for them or the post is built on a finding. Otherwise her own situation is the example. When a number is used: one, rounded the way she'd say it, in her words, with no sample size in brackets (Diana, 2026-10-09, "it sounds too much like ai"; the fourth time research lines read as AI).

## Staying recognizable over time

Diana's direction, 2026-10-09: “this is a long game” and “I also need my content to be me”.
Her subject choices, judgments and experiences matter as much as lowercase openings or punctuation.
Keep her natural uncertainty, interests, asides and recurring concerns. Do not add personality traits from a creator or a template.
A clear post needs no mandatory joke, confession, disagreement or personal scene.
Use raw notes, voice memos and eligible samples to learn. Publication metrics do not decide her voice.
A repeated edit suggests a preference in context; it does not automatically justify a universal ban.

## Character (from the BlinkWrite tone board)

Applies to Diana's posts. Where Diana's samples differ, the samples win.

- **Aim for:** sharp, perceptive, human, calm, quietly confident, slightly witty, minimal, intelligent, technical when necessary, product-aware, contemporary, helpful, effortless to read.
- **Avoid:** academic, overly emotional, passive, silly, empty, jargon-heavy, trend-chasing, overly enthusiastic, aggressive, arrogant, feature-dumping.
- **Never sound like** a generic AI company trying to impress people with technology.
- **Write about** the small, specific frustrations of modern work.
- **Not louder, not more futuristic.** Sharper, simpler, more observant.
- **Test:** could this sentence have been written by any AI startup? If yes, rewrite it.

Provisional, from 10 of Diana's raw ideas (2026-10-07; some may be AI-assisted). Replace with real posts when Diana shares them.

- Median 142 words, about 14 words per sentence (the first drafts had 8).
- Text in 1-2 blocks with single line breaks (the first drafts had 6 spaced paragraphs).

- Emoji in 2 of 10, at the end of a line.
- Asides: "Really.", "I'll be honest", "for sure", "in all honesty", "Let me explain.", "I have to say", "guess what: they care."
- Honest about herself: "I'm somewhat of a perfectionist", "for this one I am almost ashamed", "rookie mistake", "Terrible, terrible idea".
- Story posts end on the story, or on a P.S. in her words. On 2026-10-15 she removed my closing question from two: the origin story (now ends "going at full speed.") and the customer feedback (now a P.S. naming the person who was right).
