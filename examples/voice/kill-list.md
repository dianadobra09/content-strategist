# Kill list

Never in a post. `voice-check` scans every draft against this list. Add to it whenever Diana rejects something.

## Punctuation and formatting

- Em dashes (—) and en dashes (–). Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses.
- Bold or italic Unicode letters (𝗹𝗶𝗸𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀).
- Emoji as bullets or decoration (🚀 ✅ 👉 💡 🔥 👇).
- Perfect formatting: a blank line after every sentence or two, headed or numbered sections, tidy parallel bullets, bold. Diana writes in blocks. On X, one line per step is fine when it's a real sequence.
- Bullet lists for things that aren't lists.
- Hashtag blocks.
- Title Case headings inside posts.
- Exception for LinkedIn posts (Diana's edits, 2026-10-11 to 2026-10-13): the layout in `linkedin-post` step 4, with short paragraphs, blank lines between them, and a → list under a label line for a real list. Hashtags, "save this" or "send this" lines and 👇 stay out: she removed all three from ready/03 and repeated it on 2026-10-13 ("do not add the hashtags and save the...").
- Threads posts take the same short paragraphs with blank lines (`threads-post`; Diana, 2026-10-13: "nicely formatted on multiple lines").

## Openers

- "I'm thrilled / excited / humbled to share"
- "Let's talk about", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "Here's the truth", "Here's the deal:"
- "BREAKING:", "biggest ever", "(READ THAT AGAIN)"
- "Hot take:" as a label, and "Unpopular opinion:" on LinkedIn. On X and Threads, "unpopular opinion:" can open a belief Diana holds that really splits people (Diana, 2026-10-17, a test). Hot takes are fine: the claim itself is the first line (`references/reach.md`).
- "Most people think X. They're wrong." The honest version is allowed: a specific belief Diana's reader holds, then her evidence (the belief-change post in `references/psychology.md`; Diana, 2026-10-23: "include the ok raigebait").
- "Stop doing X."
- "Imagine..."
- "As a senior engineer, ..."
- A question the post then answers ("Ever wondered why...?")
- "X is dead.", except in a trend post while that format is going around (Diana, 2026-10-08; `references/reach.md`).
- "I'll be the first to admit" (Diana, 2026-10-07: "the hook does sound like ai")
- "this is my first post here", "hi, I'm Diana": no intro posts, Diana has been on LinkedIn for some time (2026-10-07). The one exception is her story in launch week, which opens on the launch (Diana, 2026-10-25).

## Constructions

- "Not X, it's Y" and its family: "It's not about X, it's about Y", "X isn't just A, it's B", "Less X, more Y"
- Lists of three for rhythm. Use the real number of items.
- Reveal lines: "The result?", "The kicker?", "The best part?", "Here's why:", "Here's what I learned:", "Spoiler:", "Plot twist:"
- Short fragments for rhythm: "Romanian is." "It takes time." "Every message, every post."
- Mirrored lines and inversions: "a missed span is a leaked span", "only then does the request go out".
- Stock drama fragments: "Then it hit me." "Game over." "Mind. Blown." A real punchline is fine ("absolutely not.").
- Line breaks for fake suspense before the point.
- "-ing" tails that add fake depth: "..., highlighting the importance of X."
- Stacked hedges: "might potentially", "it could be argued".
- Calling the same thing by three different names.
- "From X to Y" when X and Y aren't a range.
- A last line that restates the post ("The lesson? Ship small.").
- Report-summary sentences: a statistic, a comparison ("compared with"), a trend clause ("that gap has halved since...") and the sample size in brackets, all in one sentence (Diana, 2026-10-09: "it sounds too much like ai").
- Moral closers: "Remember:", "At the end of the day", "The bottom line", "Ultimately", "What works for me is...", "the other way around" (Diana, 2026-10-08: "the ending sounds like ai").

## Endings

- "Thoughts?", "Agree?", "What do you think? 👇", "Let me know in the comments"
- Generic "How are you using X?" questions. Ask something specific readers can answer about themselves.
- Either/or questions built for engagement ("do you X, or just Y?") and long, engineered questions. A closing question is short and plain, the way Diana would ask a colleague. On X, an either/or question is fine when Diana has her own answer and gives it in the replies (Diana, 2026-10-17, a test).
- "Follow me for more", "Repost if this helped ♻️", "Save this for later"
- "Comment 'X' and I'll send you..."
- A question nobody needs to answer. Diana usually ends with a real question; keep Diana's.

## Tone (from the BlinkWrite tone board)

Academic, overly emotional, passive, silly, empty, jargon-heavy, trend-chasing, overly enthusiastic, aggressive, arrogant, feature-dumping. Anything that sounds like a generic AI company trying to impress people with technology.

A newsjack, brandjack, namejack or trend post isn't trend-chasing when it carries Diana's own use case or view (`references/reach.md`).

## Worn-out topics

Reject generic versions of these topics. A concrete firsthand example, useful explanation or supported trade-off can qualify:

- AI will or won't replace engineers; "people using AI will replace you"
- Prompt tips and prompt lists
- "AI is just a tool"
- The future of work
- Vibe coding, good or bad, in general
- "I used AI to write this"
- Model A vs model B on launch day
- "Agents will change everything"; AGI timelines
- Slogans and explainers everyone has posted: "context engineering is the new prompt engineering", "MCP explained", "RAG is dead"
- Productivity hacks, "ship fast", "done is better than perfect"
- "Communication is key", "be authentic"
- Generic founder-journey lessons; "why you should build in public"
- Imposter syndrome, hustle or burnout takes without a specific story
- Reactions to someone else's launch that only restate it. A newsjack or brandjack adds a use case or consequence from Diana's work (`references/reach.md`).

## Content

- Invented stories, numbers, users, quotes or results. Hard rule.
- Vague sources: "studies show", "experts say", "many people". Every external finding needs its primary source.
- Fake vulnerability and humblebrags.
- Advice with no example ("communicate clearly", "ship fast").
- Hype or doom about AI.
- Selling BlinkWrite in the post: a hard sell, testimonial lines, a feature list or a call to action in the body. Naming it once, plainly, where it fits the post's problem is fine in every category (Diana, 2026-10-23: "allow blinkwrite promostion", "promote blinkwrite but in a non salesy way").
- Before launch: saying BlinkWrite is available, or a launch date (naming it is fine since 2026-10-16).
