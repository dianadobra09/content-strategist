# Content plan

Paths are relative to the workspace root. Read `strategy/positioning.md` for audience, mix and cadence.

## Save an idea

Save the author's exact words using `ideas/README.md`. Put attachments in `ideas/files/`.
Note available evidence and missing details without drafting or interviewing them unless the idea is unclear.
Keep research briefs distinct from the author's own statements. Confirm the saved idea in one line.

## Plan posts

Read the ready queue, the last ten published entries per platform, open ideas and open angles.
Consult used ideas and published posts for new results, failures or changed decisions.
For a follow-up, create a separate brief linked to the earlier post. Do not reuse the old idea unchanged.

Select using `generate-content` steps 3–5. Give each post one reader situation and one useful takeaway.
Make useful insights and real learnings the majority of the plan, across growth and authority posts.
For each, name the actual learning and its supporting material. Do not count a polished generic observation as substantive content.
Keep the approved queue first. Use the category mix as a planning guide, not a reason to fill a slot with weak material.
Maintain room for the author's recurring work and personal interests. Do not turn every personal post into a technical lesson.
News can be useful, but it does not automatically outrank a strong firsthand post.
Leave room for this week's conversation: when the X and Threads queues run more than about three weeks ahead, a timely post can take an evergreen post's slot (`planned_for`). Raw thoughts from the author every two or three days keep the short posts current (a creator's X and Threads playbook, `references/reach.md`).
A real follower or product milestone counts as a build-in-public post. Before a launch, the conversion slot can show the product in the author's own use, with real numbers, so launch day isn't a surprise.

For each proposed post, state its purpose, evidence, category, format and visual.
Use the author's agreed cadence. If dates are needed and cadence is unknown, ask once; otherwise give an ordered shortlist.
If evidence is missing, name what to collect. Do not create extra posts merely to meet a frequency target.
Mark a selected idea `planned`. Scheduling goes through `schedule-posts`.

## Log publication

When the author confirms publication, move each posted file from `outputs/posts/ready/<channel>/` to `outputs/posts/published/<channel>/`, keep its number and add its URL.
Set `status: published` and add a row using the schema in `outputs/log.md`.
If the published text differs, use `train-voice` to record the changes.

Collect results at 48 hours and seven days when the author provides them. Record actual observation time or post age.
Request available analytics during a review or publication follow-up; this does not create automatic reminders.
Keep missing metrics unknown. Separate the author's replies from outside responses when possible.

## Review

Treat the first ten published posts per platform as a descriptive baseline. Ready posts are not performance data.
Compare posts with similar purposes at similar ages:

- Growth: impressions, reposts and attributed follows when available.
- Authority: substantive technical questions, useful conversations and actual use of a shared resource.
- Personal: substantive responses and continuing conversations about the author's experience.
- Conversion: attributed clicks, signups or other agreed outcomes.

Breakouts: each Friday, mark the posts on every channel that reached about 4x the platform's median views that week. For each one, propose a rerun or follow-up within a week: the same idea with a new subject or angle, written fresh, plus a version written for the other platform.
Compare topics and shapes by views, follows and signups, not likes: which subjects, and questions or statements, bring new people. Likes from the same small circle aren't growth; on Threads, watch the views from non-followers. When a group responds, plan a new angle for it.

Also review voice corrections and effort: does the author recognize themselves, and is the process sustainable?
Do not copy a high-performing style they dislike. Do not infer causality from one post or small tag comparisons.
Record cautious observations under `What holds for the author` in `references/reach.md` and recommend up to three changes.
Change positioning or cadence only when the author authorizes it.
